Seasonal Pattern

Wet Winters, Dry Summers, Stressed Pipe

Charter Oak sits on the same foothill-adjacent alluvial soil as the rest of this corridor, and the community's seasonal swing between wet winters and long dry summers puts real, repeated stress on buried and slab-embedded pipe. We see a noticeable uptick in cracked pipe and service line calls here in the transition months, as the ground shifts after the first real rains following a dry stretch.

Housing History

From Citrus Orchards to a Bedroom Community

Charter Oak began as a small agricultural settlement centered on citrus orchards near the intersection of Arrow Highway and Bonnie Cove Avenue, before growing into a bedroom community through the 1960s. That history means a mix of older homes with original plumbing from the community's agricultural-era growth alongside 1960s-and-newer construction on slab foundations, each with a different typical failure pattern.

Water Supply

Golden State Water in Charter Oak

Golden State Water Company serves portions of Charter Oak alongside neighboring San Dimas and Covina, drawing on the same Main San Gabriel Basin groundwater and imported supply blend that tests hard throughout this part of the valley.

Landmarks

Arrow Highway and Bonnie Cove Avenue

The intersection of Arrow Highway and Bonnie Cove Avenue marks the original heart of the community, and the Charter Oak Unified School District, serving Charter Oak plus parts of Covina, Glendora, and San Dimas, still carries the community's name even though Charter Oak itself was never incorporated as its own city. That lack of its own city government also means residents rely on Los Angeles County directly for certain services, which we factor in when a job touches a public right-of-way or requires a county permit.

FAQs

Questions From Charter Oak

Is Charter Oak part of Glendora or Covina?

Charter Oak is its own unincorporated Los Angeles County community, situated along Arrow Highway between Covina, Glendora, and San Dimas, not officially part of any of them, though residents sometimes refer to it informally as unincorporated Covina.

Why do pipe problems seem to spike after the first rain of the season?

The alluvial soil in this area shifts as it rehydrates after a dry summer, which puts sudden stress on buried and slab-embedded pipe that's been sitting still for months, a pattern we see clearly in Charter Oak calls each year.

Does my Charter Oak home have older or newer plumbing?

It depends on which part of the community, areas closer to the original Arrow Highway and Bonnie Cove Avenue settlement tend to be older, while much of the rest reflects 1960s-and-later bedroom-community construction.

Do you charge extra to come to Charter Oak?

No, Charter Oak is inside our core service area.

How many people live in Charter Oak?

Charter Oak's population was about 9,700 at the 2020 census, making it a smaller community than the incorporated cities around it, which is part of why service calls here often involve a mix of long-time residents and newer families in the same block.

Does Charter Oak have its own city services like police or a city hall?

No, as an unincorporated community, Charter Oak relies on Los Angeles County for services that an incorporated city would otherwise provide directly, including sheriff patrol rather than its own police department.

Which school district serves Charter Oak?

Charter Oak Unified School District, established in 1894, serves the community along with portions of Covina, Glendora, and San Dimas, one of the few institutions that still carries the Charter Oak name despite the area never incorporating.

Do you cover every ZIP code in Charter Oak?

Yes. Charter Oak is served by a single ZIP code, 91724, covering the whole community along Arrow Highway from the Bonnie Cove Avenue area through to the San Dimas border. Every address in that ZIP is inside our service area.

Is emergency service available in Charter Oak?

Yes, our 24/7 emergency line covers Charter Oak the same as Glendora itself, even though it's an unincorporated community without its own city services. A burst pipe or active leak gets the same urgent response any time, day or night.
