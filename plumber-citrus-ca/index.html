A Compact, Historic Community

One of the Valley's Most Densely Built Communities

At under a square mile with over 10,000 residents, Citrus is one of the more densely built unincorporated communities in this part of the San Gabriel Valley, a legacy of its origins as citrus grove land that was subdivided into closely spaced residential lots as the surrounding area grew. That density means service calls here often involve tighter side-yard access and shared property-line plumbing runs that a more spread-out suburban tract doesn't have.

Common Calls

What We See Most in Citrus

The community's older, closely built housing stock means drain cleaning and sewer line calls are frequent, particularly where mature trees along narrow lots have sent roots into aging clay laterals. Repiping calls are also common in Citrus's original citrus-era homes, many of which still run original galvanized supply lines.

Water Supply

Shared Water Infrastructure With Its Neighbors

Citrus doesn't have its own dedicated municipal water utility, depending on the exact address, service comes through infrastructure shared with neighboring Azusa or Covina. We confirm which system serves your specific property, since it affects both water hardness and who to contact for a main-line issue.

Landmarks

From Orange Groves to Rooftops

Citrus takes its name directly from the orange and lemon groves that once covered this stretch of the valley floor before postwar development subdivided the land into the tight residential grid seen today. Dalton Park, one of the community's few open green spaces, sits within this densely built footprint and remains one of the few visible reminders of how much more open the area once was. Most of Citrus's original grove-era lots have long since been split and re-split for infill housing, which is part of why property-line plumbing runs here sometimes cross in ways a newer subdivision never would.

FAQs

Questions From Citrus

Is Citrus part of Azusa or Glendora?

Citrus is its own unincorporated Los Angeles County community between Azusa, Glendora, and Covina, though it's often informally referred to as unincorporated Azusa or unincorporated Covina depending on which side of the community you're on.

Why do sewer line issues seem so common in Citrus?

Citrus's older, closely built lots with mature landscaping create a lot of opportunity for root intrusion into aging clay sewer laterals. This is why it's one of the more frequent calls we get from this community specifically.

Does Citrus have its own water department?

No, Citrus doesn't have a dedicated municipal utility of its own, water service comes through infrastructure shared with neighboring cities depending on the exact address.

Do you charge extra to come to Citrus?

No, Citrus is inside our core service area.

How densely populated is Citrus compared to nearby cities?

Citrus packs roughly 10,000 residents into less than a square mile, making it one of the more densely built communities in this part of the valley, a real factor in how much side-yard access a technician has to work with on a given call.

Are Citrus homes mostly original construction, or has there been remodeling over the years?

It's a genuine mix, many original citrus-era bungalows remain, but decades of individual remodels and additions mean plumbing layouts can vary significantly even between adjacent lots. This is why we inspect rather than assume based on the block.

Do you need a permit for plumbing work in an unincorporated area like Citrus?

Permits for unincorporated Los Angeles County areas, including Citrus, are handled through the county rather than a city building department. We're familiar with that process and pull permits as part of the job when the work requires one.
