Common Problems

What San Dimas Homeowners Call About Most

San Dimas was largely built out after its 1960 incorporation. This means a large share of the city's housing sits on concrete slab foundations from that era, the same construction style that runs copper supply lines directly through the slab and, decades later, produces the slab leak calls we handle regularly here.

Hard water compounds the issue: water heater sediment, faucet wear, and mineral scale in drain lines show up earlier in San Dimas than they would in a softer-water region. This is why we treat water heater and softener calls as routine business here rather than the exception.

Water Supply

Golden State Water's Hard Water Blend

San Dimas has been served by Golden State Water Company since 1976, drawing on a blend of Main San Gabriel Basin groundwater with imported Metropolitan Water District and Three Valleys Municipal Water District supply. That blend tests hard, consistent with the sediment and scale pattern we see across San Dimas's water heaters and fixtures.

Local Identity

A City Built Around Bonelli Park and the Foothills

San Dimas grew rapidly after incorporation in 1960, and its proximity to Frank G. Bonelli Regional Park and the surrounding foothills means a meaningful number of homes here have larger irrigation systems, a common reason for the backflow testing calls we get from this city specifically.

Landmarks

Named for a Saint, Built Around a Reservoir

San Dimas was originally platted in the 1880s as a town called Mud Springs before being renamed after Saint Dismas, and the name has stuck through more than a century of growth. The city's most recognizable modern landmark is Puddingstone Lake inside Bonelli Park, adjacent to the Raging Waters water park, both draw significant seasonal water use that we factor into backflow and irrigation-related service calls in the surrounding neighborhoods. Homes closest to the park and the lake tend to have the largest irrigation footprints we see anywhere in our service area, which is part of why we stock backflow-testing equipment on every truck rather than dispatching it separately.
