Overview

Repiping the Inside of Your Home

Repiping replaces the water supply lines running inside your home, from the point the main service line enters the house out to every fixture, and it's a different job from replacing the service line itself, which runs from the street or meter to the house. We recommend a full or partial repipe when a home's interior plumbing has reached the point where repeated leaks, pressure problems, or water quality issues point to the pipe material itself, not an isolated failure.

Most repiping jobs in Glendora involve replacing original galvanized steel with copper or PEX, or replacing aging copper that's developed multiple pinhole leaks over the years. We plan the route to minimize wall and ceiling openings, and we patch what we do open as part of the job.

Why This Happens in Glendora

Why Older Glendora Homes Need Repiping

Homes built before the 1960s near Glendora Village and along the Route 66 corridor commonly used galvanized steel supply piping, which corrodes from the inside over decades until the interior diameter narrows dramatically and rust flakes into the water supply. Combined with the area's 150 to 220 ppm hard water, that corrosion process runs faster here than in softer-water regions. This is why we see galvanized failures in Glendora at ages where the pipe material's general lifespan would suggest it still has years left.

Copper repiping jobs are usually driven by a different pattern: multiple pinhole leaks appearing in different parts of the same copper system over a short period. That's a sign the pipe itself is reaching the end of its service life rather than any one section having an isolated defect worth patching individually.

Warning Signs

Signs Your Home Needs Repiping

Rusty or Discolored Water

A near-certain sign of internal galvanized pipe corrosion.

Multiple Pinhole Leaks

Leaks appearing in different locations over a short span of time.

Persistently Low Water Pressure

Corrosion narrowing the interior diameter of galvanized pipe.

Visible Corrosion on Exposed Pipe

Flaking, discoloration, or scale visible on pipe in a garage or basement.

Frequent Repair Calls

Repeated individual leak repairs that keep recurring elsewhere in the system.

Repair Options

Copper vs. PEX

Copper

  • Long track record and high durability
  • Resistant to damage from UV and rodents
  • Higher material and labor cost than PEX

PEX

  • Flexible, fewer joints, faster installation
  • Resistant to the pinhole corrosion pattern affecting aging copper
  • Lower overall installed cost in most homes

FAQs

Common Questions

How long does a whole-home repipe take?

Most homes are completed in a few days, with water restored to at least part of the house each night. We'll give you a specific timeline based on your home's size and layout during the assessment.

Do I need to repipe the whole house, or can it be partial?

It depends on the extent of the problem. If corrosion or leaks are isolated to one section, a partial repipe may be enough. If the pattern suggests the whole system is aging out, a full repipe is usually the more cost-effective long-term choice.

Will repiping fix my low water pressure?

If the low pressure is caused by corroded, narrowed galvanized pipe, yes, replacing it restores full flow. If the cause is elsewhere, such as the service line or a pressure regulator, we'll identify that during the assessment.

How much wall damage does repiping cause?

We plan routes to minimize openings, and any walls or ceilings we do open are patched as part of the job. It's more disruptive than a single repair, but far less invasive than most homeowners expect.
