Overview

Clogged Drains in Glendora, California

A slow or fully backed-up drain is one of the most common calls we take in Glendora, whether it's a kitchen sink that won't empty after washing dishes, a shower that pools around your feet, or a main line clog backing up into more than one fixture at once. We clear the full range, kitchen, bathroom, laundry, and main sewer line, using the method that actually matches what's causing the blockage rather than defaulting to the same tool every time.

For a single fixture clog, a cable snake usually clears the line in one visit. For a main line that keeps backing up, or one with root intrusion or years of grease and scale built up along the pipe walls, hydro jetting clears the full diameter of the pipe rather than just punching a hole through the blockage. This is why a snaked line sometimes clogs again within weeks while a jetted one doesn't.

Why This Happens in Glendora

What Clogs Glendora Drains Most Often

Glendora's older housing stock near the Village and along the Route 66 corridor often still runs original clay or older cast-iron sewer laterals, and mature landscaping, including the ficus trees the city is known for, sends roots looking for moisture at any joint or crack in those older lines. That's the leading cause of recurring main-line backups we see in the older parts of the city, and it's a clog that a simple snake will only push through, not actually clear.

The area's hard water plays a role on the fixture side too. At 150 to 220 ppm, mineral scale builds up on the inside of drain pipes over time, narrowing the effective diameter and giving grease, soap scum, and hair more surface area to grab onto, which is part of why kitchen and bathroom drains in this area tend to slow down faster than in softer-water regions.

Warning Signs

Signs You Need Drain Cleaning

Slow-Draining Sink or Tub

Water pools before draining, often the earliest sign of a building clog.

Gurgling Sounds

Air trapped behind a partial blockage, often heard in a nearby drain when another fixture runs.

Multiple Drains Backing Up

A sign the clog is in the main line, not an isolated fixture.

Recurring Clogs

The same drain clogging repeatedly often means root intrusion or heavy scale, not a one-time blockage.

Bad Odor From a Drain

Trapped organic buildup or, in some cases, a venting issue.

Water Backing Up Into a Tub or Shower

Often the first visible sign of a main sewer line clog.

Repair Options

Snaking vs. Hydro Jetting

Cable Snaking

  • Fast, effective for most single-fixture clogs
  • Lower cost for a routine blockage
  • Punches through the clog rather than clearing the full pipe wall
  • May need repeating sooner on grease or root-affected lines

Hydro Jetting

  • Clears the full diameter of the pipe, not just a channel through it
  • Best for grease buildup, scale, and root intrusion
  • Longer-lasting results on recurring main line clogs
  • Recommended after repeated snaking hasn't held

FAQs

Common Questions

Why does my drain keep clogging after it's been snaked?

A snake punches through a clog rather than clearing the full pipe wall, so grease, scale, or root intrusion often returns. Hydro jetting clears the entire diameter and typically lasts longer on recurring clogs.

Do tree roots really get into sewer lines here?

Yes, it's one of the most common causes of recurring main-line backups we see in Glendora's older neighborhoods, particularly near mature landscaping and ficus-lined streets.

Can you clear a main sewer line the same day?

In most cases, yes. We carry both snaking and hydro jetting equipment on our trucks for same-day main line service.

Is hydro jetting safe for older pipes?

We assess the pipe material and condition first, for older or already-compromised clay or cast-iron lines, snaking or a lighter jetting pressure may be the better call than a full-pressure clean.
