Overview

Filtration Solves a Different Problem Than Softening

Water filtration and water softening address different issues, and it's a common point of confusion. A softener removes hardness minerals like calcium and magnesium. A filtration system targets chlorine taste and odor, sediment, and other contaminants that hardness treatment doesn't touch. Many Glendora homes benefit from both, but they solve genuinely different problems.

We install both whole-home systems, which treat every tap in the house, and point-of-use under-sink or countertop systems for drinking and cooking water specifically. Which makes sense depends on what you're actually trying to fix, taste at the tap, sediment protecting your appliances, or something more specific.

Why This Happens in Glendora

What's in Glendora's Water Supply

Glendora's water comes from a blend of San Gabriel Valley groundwater and imported Metropolitan Water District supply, treated and disinfected before it reaches your tap. Most taste and odor complaints we hear about trace back to chlorine used in that treatment process, which a basic carbon filtration stage handles effectively.

Homes drawing more heavily on local groundwater during certain periods sometimes notice more mineral sediment than homes on a primarily imported-water blend, which is where a sediment pre-filter earns its place ahead of other fixtures and appliances, extending their service life alongside whatever hardness treatment is already in place.

FAQs

Common Questions

Do I need a softener and a filter, or just one?

It depends on what's bothering you about your water. If it's hardness, scale, soap scum, dry skin, that's a softener. If it's taste or odor, that's filtration. Many homes benefit from both, since they solve different problems.

Will a whole-home filter remove all the chlorine taste?

A properly sized carbon filtration stage handles chlorine taste and odor effectively for the vast majority of homes.

How often do filters need to be replaced?

It depends on the system and your household's water usage, typically every few months to a year for most cartridge-style filters. We'll set expectations for your specific system.

Can you install a system I've already purchased?

In many cases yes, as long as it's compatible with your existing plumbing, we can confirm during the assessment.
