Overview

Fixing a Faucet Right the First Time

A dripping faucet, a sprayer that's lost pressure, or water pooling under a sink cabinet are among the most common calls we take in Glendora. Most of the time the cause is a worn cartridge, a failed O-ring, or a loose supply-line connection, parts we carry on the truck so the repair finishes in a single visit rather than requiring a return trip for a special-order part.

We also handle full faucet replacements, whether you're upgrading a kitchen fixture during a remodel or replacing one that's corroded past the point repair makes sense. We match the new fixture to your existing sink's hole configuration or adapt the connections as needed.

Why This Happens in Glendora

Why Faucets Wear Out Faster in Glendora

At 150 to 220 ppm, the mineral content in Glendora's water supply settles inside faucet cartridges and aerators steadily over time, which is why a faucet here often starts dripping or loses pressure years before the same fixture would in a softer-water region. That scale buildup is usually what's actually failing, not the fixture's overall design or quality.

Under-sink supply line and shutoff valve failures follow a similar pattern, compression fittings and older valves accumulate mineral deposits that eventually prevent them from sealing fully, which is why a slow drip under a sink cabinet is one of the more common calls in this area's older homes near the Village.

Warning Signs

Signs You Need Faucet or Sink Repair

Constant Dripping

Usually a worn cartridge or washer, a straightforward same-visit fix.

Low Spray Pressure

Mineral scale clogging the aerator or sprayer head.

Water Under the Sink

A loose supply line connection or a failing shutoff valve.

Handle Feels Loose or Stiff

Internal cartridge wear affecting the faucet's operation.

Visible Corrosion at the Base

Can indicate a slow leak that's been going on for a while.

Old, Mismatched, or Damaged Fixture

A candidate for replacement rather than repeated repair.

Repair Options

Repair vs. Replacement

When Repair Makes Sense

  • Fixture is in good structural condition
  • Issue is a specific part, cartridge, O-ring, or aerator
  • Fixture is a newer or still-supported model

When Replacement Is the Better Call

  • Visible corrosion or pitting on the fixture body
  • Repeated repairs on the same faucet
  • Kitchen or bathroom remodel or upgrade

FAQs

Common Questions

Why does my faucet keep dripping after I've tightened it?

Tightening a faucet handle doesn't fix a worn internal cartridge or O-ring, which is almost always the actual cause of a persistent drip. It's a quick, low-cost part swap in most cases.

Can you match a new faucet to my existing sink?

In most cases, yes, we check your sink's existing hole configuration and either match it or adapt the new fixture's connections to fit.

Is water under my sink always a plumbing emergency?

A slow, contained drip can usually wait for a same-day appointment. Active flowing water or a connection that's come apart is worth calling in right away.

Do you install customer-supplied faucets?

Yes, we install faucets you've purchased as well as fixtures we provide.
