Overview

Repair, Not Automatic Replacement

A backed-up or slow-draining sewer line doesn't automatically mean the whole lateral needs to be replaced. Most of the sewer line calls we handle in Glendora turn out to be a localized problem, a cracked section, a joint separated by soil movement, or root intrusion at one specific point, that a spot repair or trenchless lining can fix without digging up the entire line.

We start every sewer line call with a camera inspection to see the actual condition of the pipe before recommending anything. If the damage is contained to a short section, we'll walk you through spot repair or trenchless lining options. If the camera shows widespread deterioration along the whole run, we'll tell you that honestly too, that's a replacement conversation, covered on our Sewer Line Replacement page.

Why This Happens in Glendora

What Damages Sewer Lines in Glendora

A large share of Glendora's housing stock, especially in the older neighborhoods near Glendora Village and along the Route 66 corridor, still runs original clay or cast-iron sewer laterals. Those materials develop cracks and joint separations over decades, and the city's mature landscaping, including its well-known ficus trees, sends roots directly into any opening looking for moisture, which is the single most common cause of localized sewer line damage we find on camera in this area.

The foothill area's alluvial soil adds a second failure mode: ground movement between wet winters and dry summers can shift a rigid clay or cast-iron line just enough to offset a joint, creating a low spot where waste and paper collect and eventually block the line, a different mechanism than root intrusion, but one that shows up just as clearly on a camera inspection.

Warning Signs

Signs of Sewer Line Damage

Recurring Backups

The same drain or multiple drains backing up repeatedly, even after clearing.

Slow Drainage Throughout the House

Multiple fixtures draining slowly at once points to the main line, not a single fixture.

Gurgling From Drains

Air trapped behind a partial blockage or offset section.

Sewage Odor Outdoors

Can indicate a crack or break allowing waste to seep into the surrounding soil.

Unusually Green or Lush Patch of Lawn

Often a sign of a leaking sewer line nourishing the grass above it.

Camera-Confirmed Root Intrusion or Cracking

The clearest confirmation, typically found during a hydro jetting or drain cleaning inspection.

Repair Options

Spot Repair vs. Trenchless Lining

Spot Repair (Dig & Replace Section)

  • Best for damage confined to one accessible location
  • Removes and replaces the specific damaged section
  • Lower cost when the dig site is easy to access

Trenchless Pipe Lining

  • Lines the existing pipe from the inside, minimal digging
  • Good option for root intrusion along a longer stretch
  • Preserves landscaping and hardscape above the line

FAQs

Common Questions

Do I need to replace my whole sewer line?

Not necessarily. Most sewer line problems we find are localized, a crack, a root intrusion point, or an offset joint, and can be spot-repaired or lined without digging up the entire lateral. A camera inspection tells us which situation you're in.

How do you know where the damage is without digging first?

A sewer camera goes directly into the line and shows us the exact location, type, and severity of the damage before we recommend anything or start any digging.

Will tree roots keep coming back after a repair?

A proper spot repair or trenchless lining seals the opening roots were entering through, which stops new intrusion at that point. Other aging sections of an older clay line can still develop new issues over time.

Is trenchless lining as durable as digging up the pipe?

Trenchless lining creates a new, jointless pipe inside the old one and holds up well for root-intrusion and crack repairs. Whether it's the right fit depends on the pipe's condition, which we confirm on camera first.
