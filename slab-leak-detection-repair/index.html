Overview

A Different Kind of Leak, a Different Repair Method

A slab leak is a leak in a water line that runs underneath your home's concrete foundation, and it needs a genuinely different diagnostic and repair approach than a leak in an accessible wall or under-sink line. You can't see it, and you generally can't hear it from inside the house the way you might notice a dripping fixture. By the time it's obvious, a warm patch on the floor, a section of flooring that's buckling, or a spike in your water bill, it's usually been running for a while.

Our approach starts with confirming it actually is a slab leak rather than a leak somewhere more accessible, using acoustic listening equipment and pressure isolation to locate the leak's position under the slab before any concrete is touched. From there, we walk through the real repair options with you, since a slab leak doesn't always mean breaking through the floor.

Why This Happens in Glendora

Why Slab Leaks Are Common in Glendora's Foothill Homes

A construction detail from Glendora's biggest growth era explains most of the slab leaks we see: builders working the foothill subdivisions from the 1970s forward ran copper water lines directly through the poured concrete slab rather than through interior walls, which was standard practice at the time but leaves those lines with nowhere to flex and no way to inspect them without cutting concrete.

The soil at the base of the San Gabriel Mountains expands and contracts with the seasons, and that movement flexes the slab and the pipe embedded in it over years of freeze-free but still-shifting ground. At the same time, Glendora's 150 to 220 ppm water hardness contributes to pinhole corrosion from the inside of the copper line, particularly at joints and elbows. Either factor alone can eventually cause a failure. Together, they're the main reason this is one of the more common calls we get from the city's older hillside subdivisions.

Warning Signs

Signs You May Have a Slab Leak

Warm Spot on the Floor

A hot-water line leak under the slab often warms the flooring directly above it.

Unexplained Water Bill Increase

Continuous water loss underground with no visible sign inside the house.

Sound of Running Water

Audible even with every fixture in the house turned off.

Damp or Warped Flooring

Carpet, laminate, or hardwood near the leak location showing moisture damage.

Low Water Pressure

Water escaping before it reaches the fixture reduces pressure throughout the home.

Cracks in the Foundation or Walls

In more advanced cases, sustained water under the slab can affect the foundation itself.

Repair Options

Slab Leak Repair Options

Direct Access (Spot) Repair

  • Breaks through the slab at the confirmed leak location only.
  • Lower cost when the leak is isolated to one spot.
  • Fastest option when access is straightforward.

Reroute (Bypass Above the Slab)

  • Runs a new line through the attic or walls, avoiding the slab entirely.
  • No concrete demolition required.
  • Often the better long-term option on an aging copper system with more leaks likely.

FAQs

Common Questions

How do I know if it's really a slab leak and not something else?

A warm patch on the floor (for a hot-water line), a water bill spike with no visible leak inside, and audible running water with fixtures off are the clearest signs. Our detection visit confirms it before any repair is quoted.

Do you have to break up my floor to fix a slab leak?

Not always. A direct spot repair does involve breaking through the slab at the leak location, but a reroute runs a new line above the slab through the attic or walls, avoiding concrete work entirely. We'll walk you through which fits your situation.

Is a slab leak covered by homeowners insurance?

Coverage varies by policy and by the cause of the leak. We can document our findings for your claim, but coverage decisions are between you and your insurer.

How urgent is a slab leak?

It depends on the severity, but sustained water under a foundation can eventually affect the slab and surrounding structure, so it's worth addressing promptly rather than waiting once it's confirmed.
