Overview

When the Whole Line Needs to Go

Sewer line replacement is a different job from sewer line repair, and we only recommend it when a camera inspection shows damage spread across enough of the line's length that spot repair or trenchless lining won't hold up, typically an older clay or cast-iron lateral with multiple root-intrusion points, several offset joints, or a pipe that's simply reached the end of its usable life.

Where the situation allows it, we use trenchless pipe bursting: a new pipe is pulled through the path of the old one, breaking it apart as it goes, which avoids trenching the entire yard or driveway. Traditional open-trench replacement is still the right call in certain situations, particularly where the existing line's path doesn't support bursting, or where other utilities are too close, and we'll tell you honestly which applies to your property.

Why This Happens in Glendora

Why Full Replacement Is Sometimes the Right Call in Glendora

The same clay and cast-iron laterals common in Glendora's older neighborhoods near the Village and along Route 66 that make localized repairs so common also, after enough decades, sometimes reach a point where root intrusion and joint separation have compromised the line at multiple points rather than one. A camera inspection that shows several distinct problem areas along the same run, rather than a single isolated defect, is what tips a recommendation from repair to replacement.

Homes in the foothill neighborhoods built from the 1970s onward occasionally face a different version of the same decision: an undersized original line that was adequate for the home as built but can't keep up after additions or fixture upgrades increased the household's actual demand on the sewer system.

Warning Signs

Signs You Need Full Replacement, Not Repair

Multiple Damage Points on Camera

Root intrusion or cracking found at several separate locations along the line.

Repeated Repairs Not Holding

A line that's already had more than one spot repair and continues to fail.

Line Age and Material

Original clay or cast-iron laterals well past their typical service life.

Persistent Backups Despite Cleaning

Jetting and repair no longer resolving the problem for more than a short period.

Undersized Line for Current Household Demand

Additions or remodels that increased fixture count beyond the original line's capacity.

Significant Pipe Bellying or Sagging

A structural low point that traps waste, which relining can't fully correct.

Repair Options

Trenchless vs. Open-Trench Replacement

Open-Trench Replacement

  • Full excavation along the pipe's path
  • Necessary when other utilities or structures block trenchless access
  • Allows direct inspection and repair of any surrounding damage

Trenchless Pipe Bursting

  • New pipe pulled through the old line's path, minimal digging
  • Preserves landscaping, driveways, and hardscape
  • Not suitable for every line path or soil condition

FAQs

Common Questions

How do I know if I need repair or full replacement?

A camera inspection is the honest way to answer this. If damage is confined to one or two accessible points, repair or lining usually makes sense. If the camera shows multiple problem areas along the whole run, replacement is the more durable choice.

Is trenchless replacement always possible?

No. It depends on the line's path, soil conditions, and how close other utilities are. We'll tell you honestly whether your property is a good candidate before recommending it.

How long does a sewer line replacement take?

A trenchless replacement is often completed in a single day. Open-trench replacement takes longer depending on line length and depth, plus any surface restoration afterward.

Will replacing my sewer line disrupt my landscaping?

Trenchless pipe bursting is specifically chosen to minimize that disruption. Open-trench work will affect the ground directly above the line's path, and we'll walk you through what to expect before starting.
