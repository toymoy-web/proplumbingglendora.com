Overview

Finding Where a Pool Is Actually Losing Water

Every pool loses some water to evaporation, but when the loss is clearly beyond what sun and wind explain, the cause is almost always a leak in the plumbing lines, the equipment pad, or a fitting somewhere in the system, not the pool shell itself, which is a less common source than most homeowners assume. We focus specifically on the plumbing and equipment side of pool leaks: the pipes running to and from the pump and filter, the fittings at skimmers and returns, and the equipment pad connections where leaks most often actually occur.

This is a genuinely common call in North Glendora, where larger foothill lots and custom estate properties carry a meaningfully higher share of private pools than the rest of the city, and where the same conditions driving slab leaks and service line failures elsewhere in Glendora apply just as directly to pool plumbing.

Why This Happens in Glendora

Why Pool Plumbing Fails in This Part of Glendora

North Glendora's foothill lots sit on the same alluvial soil that expands and contracts with the seasons throughout this corridor. Pool plumbing lines, often running underground between the equipment pad and the pool itself, take the same seasonal stress that drives slab leaks and buried service line failures in the rest of the city, a fitting or joint that's been fine for years can develop a leak after the ground shifts following the season's first significant rain.

Glendora's 150 to 220 ppm hard water adds a second factor specific to pool equipment: mineral scale builds up inside pump and filter housings and at threaded fittings, which can both mask a small leak's pressure signature and, over time, contribute to a fitting failing at a scaled, weakened point.

Warning Signs

Signs Your Pool Has a Plumbing or Equipment Leak

Water Loss Beyond Evaporation

Losing more than roughly a quarter-inch of water per day beyond normal evaporation.

Wet Spots Near the Equipment Pad

Standing water or soggy ground around the pump, filter, or heater connections.

Air Bubbles in Return Lines

Can indicate a suction-side leak pulling air into the system.

Rising Water Bill With a Pool on the Property

A pool-specific version of the same hidden-leak billing pattern we see on the house side.

Cracking or Settling Near the Pool Deck

Can indicate water escaping underground and affecting the surrounding soil.

Pump Losing Prime Repeatedly

Often points to a suction-side plumbing leak rather than an equipment malfunction.

Not Sure What You're Dealing With?

Not Sure If It's the Pool Itself or the Plumbing?

It's common to notice water loss without knowing whether the pool shell, the plumbing, or the equipment is actually responsible. A leak that only shows up when the pump is running usually points to the plumbing or equipment side, while water loss that continues even with the system off more often points to the shell or structure itself. That second category is outside what we handle directly, and we'll tell you plainly if that's what we're seeing rather than guessing.

If you're not sure which category describes your situation, describe what you're noticing when you call and we'll walk through what the detection visit will check first.

FAQs

Common Questions

How much water loss is normal for a pool?

A quarter-inch or so of evaporation per day is typical in this climate, more during hot, dry, or windy stretches. Losing noticeably more than that, especially with the pump off overnight, points to an actual leak.

Can you find a leak without draining my pool?

In most cases, yes. Pressure testing and equipment inspection can isolate a plumbing or equipment leak without draining the pool, though a leak in the shell itself may require a pool specialist's own detection method.

Do you repair pool equipment, or just the plumbing leading to it?

We handle the plumbing connections and lines to and from pool equipment. Repairing or replacing the equipment itself, such as a failed pump or heater, is outside our scope and better handled by a pool equipment specialist.

Is this service only for North Glendora?

No, we service pool plumbing leaks throughout our Glendora and east San Gabriel Valley service area. North Glendora simply has a higher concentration of the larger, pool-equipped properties where we see this call most often.
