West Covina's Postwar Growth Boom

West Covina incorporated as an independent city in 1923, but its real growth came during the postwar building boom of the 1950s and 1960s, when it became one of the fastest-growing cities in the country. That era of rapid tract-home construction means most West Covina neighborhoods run copper supply lines rather than the older galvanized piping found in some of Glendora's own oldest neighborhoods, though pockets of earlier construction near the original 1920s core still carry original galvanized lines.

With more than 109,000 residents, West Covina is the largest city in our regular service area, spanning a wide range of housing stock across its three ZIP codes.

Local Identity

Directly South of Covina

West Covina was originally formed in part to prevent the city of Covina from building a sewage facility in the area, and the two cities remain directly adjacent today. That proximity to our existing Covina coverage is exactly why West Covina fits naturally into our service area rather than requiring a separate dispatch arrangement.

Landmarks

The Lakes Shopping District and Civic Center

West Covina's Civic Center area and The Lakes shopping district anchor much of the city's commercial activity, surrounded by residential neighborhoods built predominantly during the postwar decades. The city is also home to the Shoehorn Museum, housing the world's largest collection of shoehorns, a distinctly local point of trivia for a city of its size.
