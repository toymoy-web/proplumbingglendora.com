Overview

Replacing a Water Heater the Right Way

When a water heater is beyond repair, leaking from the tank itself, corroded past the point a part can fix, or simply old enough that another failure is a matter of when, not if, a proper replacement is about more than swapping in a new unit. We size the replacement to your household's actual hot water demand, account for Glendora's hard water in how we set up and protect the new unit, and haul away the old tank as part of the job.

This is also the point where many Glendora homeowners consider switching from a traditional tank to a tankless system, or upgrading venting and connections that a straight swap wouldn't otherwise touch. We'll walk you through what actually makes sense for your home rather than defaulting to whatever was there before.

Why This Happens in Glendora

What Glendora's Water Means for a New Installation

Every water heater installed in Glendora will face the same 150 to 220 ppm hard water that shortened the life of the unit you're replacing. For a tank installation, that means we recommend a sediment trap and set a realistic flush schedule from day one, rather than letting the new unit follow the same wear pattern. For tankless installations, it means sizing a unit with adequate flow capacity and recommending a maintenance descale schedule that actually matches this area's mineral load, not a generic manufacturer default.

We also see this as the right moment to correct issues left over from the original installation: an undersized gas line, an outdated venting setup on an older Craftsman-era home near the Village, or a code-non-compliant expansion tank. Fixing those now means the new unit isn't set up to fail the same way the old one did.

Warning Signs

Signs It's Time to Replace, Not Repair

Tank Is Actively Leaking

Water at the base of the unit from the tank shell itself, not a fitting, means the tank has failed.

Unit Is 10+ Years Old

Most tank water heaters in hard-water areas reach the end of their practical life in this range.

Rust-Colored Water

A sign of internal tank corrosion that repair can't reverse.

Repeated Repairs

If you've called for repair more than once in the past year or two, replacement is often the more economical path.

Not Enough Hot Water

A household that's outgrown its water heater's recovery capacity.

Considering a Tankless Upgrade

Endless hot water and a smaller footprint are common reasons to switch during a planned replacement.

Repair Options

Tank vs. Tankless

Traditional Tank

  • Lower upfront cost.
  • Simpler installation in most existing setups.
  • Hot water supply limited by tank size.
  • Typical lifespan of 8 to 12 years in this area's hard water.

Tankless

  • Continuous hot water on demand.
  • Smaller footprint, wall-mounted.
  • Lower standby energy use.
  • Needs a regular descale schedule given local water hardness.

FAQs

Common Questions

How long does a water heater installation take?

Most straightforward tank replacements are completed same-day. A tankless conversion or an installation requiring gas line or venting changes may take longer, we'll give you a clear timeline during the assessment.

Is tankless worth it given Glendora's hard water?

Tankless units perform well here as long as they're on a regular descale schedule. We'll set that schedule based on your actual water hardness rather than a generic manufacturer default.

Do you haul away my old water heater?

Yes, removal and disposal of the old unit is included with every installation.

What size water heater do I need?

It depends on your household size and hot water usage patterns, not just the size of the unit you're replacing. We size it correctly during the in-home assessment.
