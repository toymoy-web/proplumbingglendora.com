Duarte's History Runs Along Route 66

Duarte traces its name back to Corporal Andres Duarte's original 1841 Mexican land grant, and historic Route 66 runs directly through the city, the same alignment that passes through Glendora's own stretch of the highway. Homes built along and near that corridor during the highway's active decades tend to carry original galvanized supply piping, consistent with the same construction era we see on our own side of this corridor.

Duarte's official motto, "City of Health," comes from its history as a destination for people seeking a mild climate, and from the medical institutions, including City of Hope, that were established here in the early 20th century. Newer residential construction surrounding those older neighborhoods more typically runs copper supply lines instead.

Hard Water Across the Foothill Corridor

Duarte sits within the same general San Gabriel Valley water supply picture as Glendora and its other immediate neighbors, drawing on a mix of local groundwater and imported supply that commonly tests in the moderately hard to hard range. That mineral load drives the same sediment buildup in water heaters and scale on fixtures that we treat throughout this corridor.

Local Identity

Bordering Azusa Along the Foothills

Duarte sits directly between Azusa and Monrovia at the base of the San Gabriel Mountains, close enough to our existing Azusa coverage that it fits naturally into the same service corridor. Like Azusa, the alluvial soil here shifts seasonally, contributing to the same cracked and ruptured pipe calls we see throughout the foothill communities.

Landmarks

City of Hope and the Route 66 Corridor

City of Hope National Medical Center, one of the country's leading cancer research and treatment centers, anchors a large section of Duarte and brings a mix of medical-campus-adjacent housing and longtime residential neighborhoods. Along the historic Route 66 alignment through town, older commercial and residential buildings reflect the same mid-20th-century construction standards found on Glendora's own stretch of the highway.
