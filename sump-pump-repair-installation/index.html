Overview

Sump Pumps for Homes Without Basements

Most Glendora homes don't have basements, so sump pumps aren't always the first thing homeowners here think about. But older homes near Glendora Village with raised, crawl-space foundations can still take on standing water during a heavy rain event, and foothill lots in North Glendora often see hillside runoff drain toward the house instead of away from it. A sump pump addresses both situations directly.

We install and repair sump pumps for crawl space dewatering, hillside drainage, and properties with an existing sump pit that needs service.

Why This Happens in Glendora

Crawl Spaces and Foothill Runoff

Glendora's older housing stock near the Village and along the Route 66 corridor was commonly built with a raised foundation and a crawl space rather than a slab, a construction style that keeps the living area above grade but leaves an open space underneath that can collect water during the season's heavier storms. Without a working drainage system, that water sits against the subfloor and framing, which leads to moisture, odor, and eventually structural problems.

North Glendora's foothill lots face a different version of the same issue. Homes built against the slope of the San Gabriel Mountains can see rainwater running downhill toward the structure during an atmospheric river event, especially on properties where the original grading or a French drain system was never designed for the volume of runoff a bigger storm brings.

Both situations call for the same basic fix: a sump pump installed at the lowest point, set up to move water away from the structure before it causes damage.

Warning Signs

Signs You Need Sump Pump Service

Standing Water in the Crawl Space

Visible pooling after rain is the clearest sign drainage isn't keeping up.

Musty Odor From Under the House

Often the first sign of moisture building up in a crawl space.

Existing Pump Running Constantly

Can indicate a failing check valve or a pump that's undersized for the job.

Pump Makes Noise But Doesn't Discharge Water

Usually a clogged discharge line or a worn impeller.

Hillside Runoff Pooling Near the Foundation

A sign that grading or an existing drain system isn't handling storm flow.

No Backup Power on an Existing System

A pump with no battery backup stops working during an outage, exactly when it's needed most.

Repair Options

Repair vs. New Installation

When Repair Makes Sense

  • Pump is under 7 years old.
  • Single component failure, like a float switch or check valve.
  • Basin and discharge line are still in good condition.

When New Installation Is the Better Call

  • No existing sump system and recurring water intrusion.
  • Pump motor has failed or is undersized for the property.
  • Upgrading to add battery backup for outage protection.

FAQs

Common Questions

Do Glendora homes actually need sump pumps if there's no basement?

Most Glendora homes don't have basements, but older homes near the Village with raised, crawl-space foundations can still take on standing water during heavy rain, and foothill lots with hillside runoff face a similar risk. A sump pump addresses both situations even without a basement.

How do I know if I actually need a sump pump?

The clearest signs are standing water or persistent dampness in a crawl space after rain, a hillside lot where runoff drains toward the house instead of away from it, or an existing sump pit that came with the home. We can assess your specific property before recommending one.

What happens if the power goes out during a storm?

A battery backup system keeps the pump running during an outage, which matters most during the exact atmospheric river events when flooding risk is highest. We can add backup to a new or existing system.

How often does a sump pump need maintenance?

We recommend a test and inspection before the start of the rainy season each year, checking the float switch, discharge line, and backup power if installed, so it's ready before it's actually needed.

Do you work on existing sump pumps, or only new installations?

Both. We repair failed float switches, clogged or frozen discharge lines, and worn-out pumps, and we install new systems in crawl spaces or hillside properties that don't have one yet.
