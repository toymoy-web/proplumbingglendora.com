Overview

Finding Leaks You Can't See

Most of the leak calls we get in Glendora don't start with a visible pipe break, they start with a water bill that jumped for no obvious reason, a water meter that's still spinning with every fixture in the house turned off, or a damp patch on a wall or floor with no clear source. That's exactly the situation electronic leak detection is built for: finding where water is escaping without cutting open walls or digging up the yard on a guess.

We use acoustic listening equipment and pressure testing to isolate a leak to a specific section of pipe before any repair work starts. For a home on a slab foundation, that often means confirming whether the leak is actually under the slab or coming from somewhere more accessible, like a fitting behind a fixture or a supply line inside a wall, a distinction that changes both the repair method and the cost.

Why This Happens in Glendora

Why Glendora Homes Develop Hidden Leaks

Glendora sits at the base of the San Gabriel Mountains on alluvial soil that expands and contracts with the seasons, wet winters followed by long dry stretches. That soil movement puts steady stress on underground service lines and slab-embedded plumbing, and it's a documented contributor to the cracked and ruptured pipe calls this area sees more of than flatter, more stable ground nearby.

The city's hard water compounds the problem. At 150 to 220 ppm, mineral deposits build up inside older copper and galvanized lines, and that buildup can mask a small leak's pressure signature or, in older soldered copper joints common in homes near Glendora Village, actually contribute to the joint failing years before it should. Homes built from the 1970s onward in the foothill neighborhoods tend to see more slab leaks specifically, since that era's construction ran copper supply lines directly through the concrete slab.

Warning Signs

Signs You May Have a Hidden Leak

Rising Water Bill

No change in usage, but the bill keeps climbing month over month.

Meter Still Moving

Every fixture off, but the water meter dial keeps turning.

Warm Spot on the Floor

A common early sign of a hot-water slab leak.

Damp Wall or Ceiling

Discoloration or soft drywall with no plumbing fixture directly above it.

Mold or Musty Smell

Persistent moisture behind a wall or under flooring, even without visible water.

Low Water Pressure

A drop in pressure at one or more fixtures can point to a supply-line leak.

Not Sure What You're Dealing With?

Not Sure If It's a Slab Leak, a Fixture Leak, or Something Else?

It's common to notice a symptom, a warm patch on the floor, a meter that won't stop turning, water pooling near the foundation, without knowing what's actually causing it or which service you need. That's normal, and it's exactly what the detection visit is for. A warm or wet spot on a slab floor usually points toward a slab leak specifically, which is diagnosed and repaired differently from a leak in an accessible wall or under-sink line. A meter that keeps spinning with everything off points to the service line between the street and the house rather than anything inside.

If you're not sure which of those describes your situation, don't worry about diagnosing it yourself before you call, describe what you're seeing and we'll tell you what the detection visit will check first.

FAQs

Common Questions

Do you have to cut into my wall or slab to find a leak?

Not to find it. Acoustic and pressure-testing equipment lets us narrow the location without opening anything up first. Cutting or digging only happens for the confirmed repair.

How do I know if it's a slab leak?

A warm patch on the floor (for a hot-water line leak) or a persistently damp area on a slab-on-grade home are the two most common signs. A detection visit confirms it either way.

Why does my water meter keep spinning with everything off?

That almost always points to a leak on the service line between the meter and the house, rather than a fixture inside, a detection visit will confirm the exact section.

Is leak detection covered if I have a home warranty?

That depends on your specific policy. We can provide documentation of our findings for you to submit, but coverage decisions are between you and your provider.
