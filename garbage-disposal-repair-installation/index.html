Overview

Disposal Problems We Handle Most Often

A garbage disposal that hums but won't spin, jams repeatedly, or leaks from underneath the sink is one of the more common kitchen calls we get in Glendora. Most humming units have a jam that a proper reset and clearing resolves, while a unit that's silent entirely often needs an electrical or motor diagnosis rather than a simple unjam.

Leaks from a disposal usually come from one of three places: the sink flange seal, the dishwasher connection, or the discharge pipe connection to the drain, and each has a different fix. We also handle new installations, whether you're replacing a failed unit or upgrading to a higher-horsepower model.

Why This Happens in Glendora

What Causes Most Disposal Failures Here

Glendora's 150 to 220 ppm hard water contributes to disposal wear in a less obvious way than it does with faucets or water heaters: mineral buildup on the internal components can add resistance to the motor over time, which shows up as more frequent jamming or a unit that runs hotter and trips its reset button more often than it should.

In older homes near Glendora Village, disposal units are sometimes original to a kitchen remodel done decades ago, well past the typical service life of any disposal regardless of water quality. At that point, a repair often costs close to what a new unit would, especially factoring in the labor to access aging connections.

Warning Signs

Signs You Need Disposal Repair or Replacement

Humming, Not Spinning

Usually a jam that a proper reset and manual clearing resolves.

Completely Silent

Can point to an electrical issue or a motor that's failed entirely.

Leaking Underneath

Often the sink flange seal, dishwasher connection, or discharge pipe.

Frequent Jamming

Recurring jams can point to mineral buildup or a unit reaching the end of its life.

Unusual Grinding Noise

Worth checking for anything that shouldn't be in the disposal before it damages the motor.

Bad Odor That Won't Clear

Trapped food debris that a standard cleaning hasn't resolved.

Repair Options

Repair vs. Replacement

When Repair Makes Sense

  • Unit is under 6 to 8 years old
  • Issue is a jam, seal, or connection point
  • Motor still runs when the jam is cleared

When Replacement Is the Better Call

  • Motor has failed or won't reset
  • Unit is old enough that parts are hard to source
  • Kitchen remodel or upgrade to a higher-horsepower model

FAQs

Common Questions

My disposal hums but won't turn. What's wrong?

That's almost always a jam. Most units have a manual reset and an Allen-wrench turning point underneath for clearing it, we can walk you through it, or handle it during a same-day visit.

Is it worth repairing an old disposal?

It depends on the unit's age and what's actually wrong. A seal or connection repair is usually worth it on a newer unit; a motor failure on an older disposal is often close in cost to just replacing it.

Can you install a disposal we've already purchased?

Yes, we install customer-supplied disposals as well as units we provide.

Why does my disposal leak only when the dishwasher runs?

That points to the dishwasher drain connection on the disposal rather than the unit itself, a specific, usually straightforward fix once confirmed.
